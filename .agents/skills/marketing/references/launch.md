# Launch

The day a product is shown to people on purpose. Planned in a file, approved
by the maintainer, and followed by a week of listening.

## Ready, before a date is set

- A stranger can go from first visit to the thing they came for, end to end,
  on a real phone or machine. Run it; do not read about it.
- The site, the store listing and the legal pages are live and say the same
  thing.
- The context file exists, and the copy follows it.
- People can reach you: a support address or page, and someone reading it on
  the day.
- The numbers to watch are named, and the data analyst knows where they come
  from.

## Soft first, then public

1. **Soft launch:** friends, a small community, one city, one market. It
   finds what is obviously broken while it is cheap.
2. Fix what it found.
3. **Public launch:** the plan below.

## The plan file

`brain/06-marketing/YYYY-MM-DD-launch-topic.md`, approved before the day:

| Field | Says |
|---|---|
| Date and time | in the audience's time zone |
| Audience | from the context file |
| The one message | one sentence, the same everywhere |
| Channels | owned (the site, a mailing list, the product's accounts); earned (communities, directories, press); paid (`ads.md`) |
| Per channel | the post or asset, written in that place's own norms, and when it goes out |
| On the day | who answers comments and support, for how long |
| What counts as success | the numbers and their targets for the first week |

## Rules

- Write for each community the way it talks; read its rules first. One honest
  post, never the same text pasted everywhere.
- Be there on the day to answer. The first questions decide what people think.
- Nothing posted, sent or paid without the maintainer's yes.

## After

A week later: what happened against the targets, what people asked for, what
broke. Into the plan file and the journal; the numbers go to the data analyst,
the requests to the product manager.
