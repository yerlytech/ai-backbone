# Copy

Words that make the right person say "that is for me", and do the next thing.

## Rules

- **The reader's outcome first, the feature second.** "Never miss a bill again"
  before "automatic payment reminders".
- **Their words, not ours.** Take them from the context file's "Words they
  search" and from how people describe the problem, never from the codebase.
- **One idea per headline.** If it needs "and", it is two headlines.
- **Specific beats impressive.** A number, a place, a time, a concrete noun.
  "Two minutes" beats "fast"; "fast" beats "seamless".
- **Proof next to the promise:** what it does, shown; a count that is true; a
  screenshot. Never an invented review or user.
- **Cut a third.** Then read it aloud. What you stumble on, rewrite.
- **Write in the language, do not translate into it.** Each language gets its
  own headline, idioms and length.

## Words to avoid

Superlatives with no proof (best, revolutionary, ultimate), filler (seamless,
robust, cutting-edge, leverage, empower), and anything the voice section of
the context rules out.

## A landing page, top to bottom

| Block | Job |
|---|---|
| Headline | who it is for and what they get, in under ten words |
| Subhead | how, in one sentence |
| Call to action | one verb and what follows ("Download free", "Book a demo") |
| Proof | a real number, a screenshot, a short real quote with permission |
| How it works | three steps, each a verb |
| Objections | the three questions people really ask, answered |
| Call to action again | the same one |

One page, one call to action. A second offer goes on a page of its own.

## A store listing

- The name and subtitle carry the main search words and the promise (see
  `search.md` for the fields and their limits).
- The first lines of the description are all most people read: the promise,
  then three reasons, then the details.
- Screenshot captions are headlines: one benefit each, readable at phone size.
  The first two do most of the work.
- Release notes are read: say what changed for the user, in their words.

## Messages

Short, one action, sent when it helps the user (a reminder they asked for, a
reply to them), never because the calendar says so. Every message says how to
stop them.
